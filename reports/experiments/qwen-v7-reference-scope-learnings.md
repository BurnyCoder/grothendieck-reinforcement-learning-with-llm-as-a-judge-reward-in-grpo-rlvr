## What the intervention tested

The earlier attempt assigned novelty to an ordinary parameterized probability
calculation and withheld it from brief commutator connections. Its reference
descriptions left the relevant mathematical domains implicit. Stating those
domains explicitly was a narrow input intervention, chosen to preserve the
user's modest-credit preference and the measurement rules. It was not assumed
to repair either error, and no calibration answers or expected scores were
added to the references.

The ordinary probability control now received no novelty, while its supported
theory retained credit. Its initial assessment omitted evidence for a positive
criterion and was rejected; the bounded retry produced an accepted assessment.
The decorated probability control also received no novelty. These observations
are a limited development improvement, not evidence that arbitrary conventional
generalizations will be handled correctly. The retry is part of the instrument;
this comparison does not isolate a particular phrase as the cause.

## Remaining failures

The brief commutator controls still received no credited novelty. The judge
identified the connection, but classified the response by the conventional
calculation used for the original problem or demanded a developed use. This
disagrees with the specified modest-credit rule. In one assessment, a positive
raw novelty score coexisted with a within-reference declaration, so code withheld
it. The archived opinion remains unchanged.

The later brief GCD/subgroup control received the same novelty and higher total
theory quality than the developed reachable-lattice framework. Its
`use_evidence` quoted the ordinary Euclidean invariant, while its rationale
treated the subgroup connection as explaining that invariant. In the candidate,
the conventional argument comes first and supports the brief subgroup link;
the quoted invariant does not demonstrate a further use of that added link.
Because the excerpt was exact and nonempty, the declared-use gate permitted
the higher novelty cap. This is a semantic evidence-attribution failure that
substring validation cannot detect. It does not establish a mathematical error
in the candidate's subgroup statement.

The final contrast therefore failed both the total-quality ordering and the
strict novelty ordering. Earlier commutator failures concerned control-credit
conditions instead. Reporting only higher framework scores would conceal these
different errors. General-theory comparisons and deliberately false-theory cases
were not reached; no suppression result can be inferred.

## Interpretation and remaining work

The isolated reference change did not qualify the combined judge-and-code
instrument. Exact excerpts and deterministic score caps enforce an output
contract, but do not establish that an excerpt supports the relationship claimed
for it. This limitation is consistent with the distinction made in the
[judge-method review](../judge-inference-review.md); no external result is used
to substitute for this failed local test.

Keep the user's modest-credit rule, cases, thresholds and every historical result.
The remaining development variants, untouched audits and long training stay
blocked under this calibration sequence. No unchanged failed identity will be
rerun for a preferred outcome. A further attempt needs a dated, published
intervention that addresses both reference comparison and semantic attribution,
with the same acceptance gates. Further prompt edits alone have not been shown
to solve these problems.

These are constructed development expectations, with reused mathematical
templates and no independent human preference labels. They do not establish
general novelty-judging ability, policy improvement, historical discovery or a
latent-space mechanism. All comparative, OOD and recipient-transfer results
remain unmeasured; the implemented training pipeline is not evidence of success.
