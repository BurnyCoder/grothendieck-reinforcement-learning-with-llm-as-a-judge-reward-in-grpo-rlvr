# Gemma v4 development calibration: failed novelty discrimination

Date: 2026-11-15. This is development evidence, not a comparative policy result.

The passive-waiting runtime completed the observed structured requests without
a transport or evidence-contract failure, but the fixed novelty test became
unreachable. The calibration therefore stopped before the general-theory and
false-theory blocks. The computed rates, counts and scores are in the
[generated case report](calibration-evaluation_judge-quoted-dev-ef8a300c.md).
Full requests, responses and exact excerpts are preserved in
[the event log](../development/gemma-passive-v4-dev.jsonl); the immutable result
is [saved separately](../development/calibration-gemma-passive-v4-dev.json).

The judge preferred the developed mathematical bridges on total theory quality.
However, the protocol also requires the conventional controls to receive zero
novelty. That condition failed for the polynomial and probability controls.
The polynomial control mentions the noncommutative boundary of the difference
of squares identity. The judge treated that remark as an additional connection.
The boundary statement is mathematically valid; its novelty classification is
debatable relative to the compact reference. This disagreement is not evidence
that the judge made an algebraic error.

The probability control applies independence to even/odd events with arbitrary
probabilities. The judge credited this ordinary parameterized calculation as
novel, despite the reference including independence and the rubric excluding
routine parameter substitutions. Its rationale discussed enumeration and
polynomials without accounting for the full supplied reference. This provides
a concrete example of the rubric's reference-comparison instruction not being
followed reliably.

Evidence-first output and exact-excerpt validation can prevent fabricated
supporting quotations; they do not ensure that the cited mathematics deserves
the selected score. The observation motivates checking conceptual classification
separately from output-contract compliance. It does not justify repairing the
scores after the fact, removing failed cases, or lowering the acceptance gate.

Passive waiting did not reproduce the prior severe slowdown during this limited
attempt. Because the mathematical futility rule ended the run early, sustained
serving reliability remains unestablished. The runtime intervention is documented
in [its prior amendment](../amendments/2026-11-15-judge-stream-deadline.md).
Different judgments across attempts do not establish a causal effect of the
waiting setting; no bitwise inference determinism was promised.

Next, run the already implemented Qwen v4 development configuration and inspect

Any subsequent general protocol revision requires a dated amendment, published
code, new calibration identities and separate development reporting. A failure
to validate the reward instrument leaves the proposed behavioral improvement
untested; it is not a negative estimate of the treatment effect.
